Multiplayer guide: Quick Match, private rooms and fair play

Racing is where PuggieType differs most from a plain typing test. This guide covers the three ways to race, what happens during a race, and the rules that keep results fair.

Before you start

Multiplayer lives on the race page. You don't need an account. The first time you open it, you are asked to confirm that you are 13 or older; multiplayer is not available to younger players. Your browser then connects to our game server and you are given a random nickname such as "ZippyPug42". You can't type your own name, which keeps rooms free of offensive names, but you can reroll it if you don't like the one you got. There is no chat and no way to message other players.

Three ways to race

Private rooms

Create a room and you become its host. The room gets an 8-character code made of letters and digits that are hard to confuse (no 0, O, 1, I or L), and an invite link of the form puggietype.com/r/CODE. Use the "copy invite" button and send the link to the people you want to race; opening it takes them straight into your lobby. Anyone with the link or code can join while the room is open, so share it only with people you want in the race. Private rooms are a good fit for a group of friends, a class warm-up or a quick contest at work.

The host chooses the race settings:

  • a timed race of 15, 30, 60 or 120 seconds, or a race of 10, 25, 50 or 100 words;
  • punctuation and numbers, each on or off;
  • the word language.

The default is a 30-second English race without punctuation or numbers. For more on what these options change, see modes and settings.

Open Rooms

A host can list their room publicly in Open Rooms. The Open Rooms list shows each listed room's code, its settings, how many seats are taken out of the maximum, and roughly how long ago it was opened. It never shows nicknames. Anyone can join a listed room, so it is a simple way to find a game when you don't have people of your own to invite.

Quick Match

Quick Match puts you in a race with strangers near your speed, with no setup. Quick Match races always use the same settings, 30 seconds of English words without punctuation or numbers, so every result is comparable.

To find you a fair opponent, your browser sends the average speed of your last 10 valid solo tests with those same settings. If you have none yet, it assumes 50 wpm. As you race, the server also builds its own estimate of your speed from your races and leans on that, so the matching gets more accurate the more you play. Matching starts with players in your own speed band and widens over the first 15 seconds. A race begins as soon as enough similar players are waiting. If nobody suitable turns up within about 15 seconds, the race is filled with Pug Bots so you are never left waiting.

A good habit before your first Quick Match: take a few solo 30-second English tests on the home page. That gives matchmaking a real number instead of the 50 wpm guess.

Pug Bots

Pug Bots are computer-controlled racers. They are always named "Pug Bot · ~NN wpm", where the number is the pace they aim for, and they always carry a visible "Bot" label. Their typing is generated by the server, with small speed variations and the occasional mistake, and is not based on any recorded player. Bots never count as people in player counts and can't be reported.

How a race runs

  1. Lobby. Players join and press ready. The lobby shows who is in, who is ready and who is host. The host can start once there are at least two players and everyone else is ready.
  2. Countdown. A synchronized 3-2-1 countdown runs on every screen. Your keyboard is locked until it reaches zero, so nobody gets a head start.
  3. Race. Everyone types the same words, chosen by the server. You see each opponent's caret moving through the text with their name, progress bars for everyone, and each player's dog trotting faster as they speed up. The progress bars stay visible even while the rest of the page fades out to keep you focused.
  4. Podium. When the race ends, a podium shows each player's place, speed, raw speed and accuracy. In timed races, places are decided by speed; in word races, by who finished first. The host can start a rematch with the same room, or anyone can leave.

If your connection drops, you have 20 seconds to reconnect before your seat is given up. Leaving a race with the browser's back button asks for confirmation first, because you can't rejoin a race in progress.

Fair play

Racing is only fun if results are real, so several protections run in the background:

  • The server checks every result. It receives what each player typed and recomputes their speed against the shared word list. It flags results whose claimed speed doesn't match, speeds above 300 wpm, progress that jumps faster than a human can type, and finishes before the start.
  • Paste is blocked. You can't paste, copy or select text in the typing area.
  • Same rules for everyone. Settings that change the difficulty or end a test early, such as funboxes, stop on error and minimum speed, are switched off during a race.
  • Reporting. You can report a player in your room for cheating, being away from the keyboard, an offensive name or something else. You choose a reason from a list; there is no free text.
  • Hosts can remove players. A host can kick a player from the lobby or the results screen. That player can't rejoin the same room for 10 minutes.

Players who are reported for cheating by several different people, or who are repeatedly flagged by the server's checks, are matched only with Pug Bots in Quick Match for the rest of their session. Rooms close when everyone has left or after an hour without activity.

What is stored

Race results are not saved to any account or leaderboard. Nicknames, room codes and race data are held in the game server's memory only while the room exists. Player reports are kept for 7 days without the reporter's identity. The privacy policy has the full list, and the terms describe what isn't allowed.

Common problems

  • Can't join a room. The room may have closed, be full (eight players), or already be racing; wait for the race to end and try again. If you typed the code by hand, check it: codes never contain 0, O, 1, I or L.
  • Can't start. Everyone except the host needs to be ready, and the room needs at least two players.
  • Matched with bots only. Few players at your speed were online during the 15-second window. Try again in a few minutes.

Something else not working? The FAQ covers more cases, and the contact page explains what to send us.
