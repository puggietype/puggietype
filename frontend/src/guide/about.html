About PuggieType

PuggieType is a free typing test that you can open and start using in a second, with multiplayer races built in and a small pug that types along with you.

What it is

At its core PuggieType is a typing test. You open the home page, the words are already there, and the timer starts with your first keypress. When you finish, you get your speed in words per minute, your raw speed, your accuracy, your consistency and a chart of how your speed changed second by second. There is no sign-up, no account and no waiting screen between you and the next test. Press tab then enter and a fresh test is ready.

The default test is 30 seconds of common English words with no punctuation or numbers, which is a good middle ground: long enough to give a stable number, short enough to repeat a dozen times in a few minutes. From there you can switch to timed tests of 15, 30, 60 or 120 seconds, fixed word counts, real quotes, an untimed zen mode, or your own text. The modes and settings guide walks through each one.

Built for racing other people

Most typing tests are something you do alone. PuggieType treats racing as a first-class feature rather than an add-on. From the race page you can:

  • create a private room and send the invite link or 8-character code to friends, classmates or coworkers;
  • join Quick Match, which puts you in a race with people near your own speed;
  • browse Open Rooms, public lobbies that anyone can join.

Everyone in a race types the same words, sees the other players' progress live, and ends up on a podium with their place, speed and accuracy. Rooms hold up to eight players and need no account: the server hands you a random pug-themed nickname for the session. There is no chat, so a room is only ever about the race. You can read how matchmaking, invite links and fair play work in the multiplayer guide.

The pug

The pug in the logo is also on the test page. While a test is running, it sits under the words and taps its paws in time with your keypresses: left, right, sometimes both. It blinks now and then. If you push past roughly 130 words per minute it starts to heat up, its face warms, it shakes harder the faster you go, and it eventually starts to steam. It is a small thing, but it gives you a feel for your pace without looking at a number. If you would rather type without company, turn off the "pug" option under appearance in the settings. The pug also follows your reduced-motion preference.

The site has its own themes too. The default, puggie dark, uses warm browns and a soft orange accent; puggie light and midnight chow sit next to well over a hundred other color themes you can pick from.

What we care about

Measuring honestly

The numbers on the result screen follow published, fixed rules: five characters count as one word, only correctly typed words count toward your speed, and every wrong keypress lowers your accuracy even if you fix it later. We explain the exact formulas, with worked examples, in how WPM and accuracy are calculated.

Privacy by default

Solo tests run entirely in your browser. Your settings and results are stored in your browser and never sent to us; the only exception is Quick Match, which sends your average speed and a random Quick Match ID when you join. Multiplayer needs a connection to our game server, and nicknames, rooms and race results are kept in memory only while your session or room exists. Player reports are kept for 7 days and then deleted. There are no analytics or tracking scripts. The full details are in the privacy policy.

Getting better, not just getting a score

A test is only useful if it helps you improve. Settings such as stop on error, confidence mode, minimum accuracy and the pace caret turn the test into a practice tool. Our typing tips page has a practice plan that uses them.
