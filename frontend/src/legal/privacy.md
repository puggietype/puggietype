# Privacy Policy

_Effective date: November 20, 2026. Last updated: November 24, 2026._

## Who we are
PuggieType (puggietype.com) is responsible for the personal data described in this policy. For any privacy question or request, email privacy@puggietype.com.

## Solo typing tests
The solo typing test needs no account. Your settings, themes, custom text, tags and test results are stored only in your browser (localStorage). If you turn on Ghost Race, your browser also keeps a replay of your personal best for each test setup (key "puggie-ghost-race"): your speed, accuracy, the date, how long the run lasted, and a timing of how many characters you had correct — not the text you typed, and no account or device identifier. A second key ("puggie-ghost-race-enabled") remembers that the feature is on. This stays on your device only. You can delete it with "clear ghosts" in Settings, or by clearing site data. For Quick Match, your browser also keeps a short list (key "puggie-mp-recent-pace") of the speed, test mode, length and language of your last 10 solo 30-second English tests, with no dates or text; it is only used to send your average speed when you join Quick Match. Your browser also keeps a random Quick Match ID (key "puggie-mp-client-id", in localStorage and IndexedDB). It isn't linked to your nickname or account and is sent to our server only when you join Quick Match, so we never match you against yourself (for example, from a second tab). The server holds it in memory only while you're in the queue and never logs or stores it. Apart from that average speed, this ID, a leaderboard score you choose to submit (see Leaderboards), and the anonymous solo test count and, if you accept analytics, the limited test details described under Cookies, analytics and error reporting, this data never leaves your device unless you export it yourself or sign in to an account (see Accounts (ages 13+)). Clearing your browser data deletes them. Some optional features make your browser contact a third party directly, which then receives your IP address: opening the release notes (GitHub's public API), the poetry and Wikipedia text modes (poetrydb.org and Wikipedia), and an optional easter-egg video (YouTube, which may set its own cookies).

## Multiplayer (ages 13+)
Multiplayer has three modes: **private lobbies** (join by invite link or code), **Open Rooms** (public lobbies anyone can browse and join), and **Quick Match** (we match you with players near your speed). When you use multiplayer, our game server at ws.puggietype.com processes:
- **a random nickname** that the server assigns (you can't type your own);
- **the lobby code** and lobby settings;
- **race data**: progress, speed, accuracy and results;
- **connection data**: your IP address (needed to connect you, and to apply connection and spam limits), and a random player ID and session token.

**Who sees what.** Your nickname and race data are shown to the players in your room. Anyone with a private lobby's invite link or code can join it while it is open. This includes up to 60 seconds after everyone has left. Lobbies are private unless the host chooses to list them in Open Rooms. A listed lobby is public: anyone can see its code and join it. Quick Match rooms are public in the same way. The public Open Rooms list shows each listed lobby's code, its race settings, how many players and bots are in it out of the maximum, and roughly how long ago it was opened (for example "opened < 5 min ago"). It also shows a total of players racing right now, which counts people only, never bots. It doesn't show nicknames or anything else about the players.

**Speed matching (Quick Match).** To match you with players near your speed, your browser sends the server the average speed of your last 10 solo tests in the Quick Match setup (30 seconds, English), taken from a small list kept in your browser (see above). If you have no such tests, it sends 50 wpm. During your session, the server also keeps a verified speed based on your multiplayer races. We use these only to match you with similar players, and we also use your IP address to apply Quick Match rate limits. This matching speed is not a leaderboard score. It is kept in memory for your session and never saved.

**Quick-chat.** When you send quick-chat, our server checks that the preset is on our approved list and relays only that preset's ID, with the random player ID for your current session (which your lobby already uses to show who's who), to the players in your lobby. We don't store quick-chat, and we don't write it to our logs. We keep three anonymous daily totals: how many quick-chat lines and emotes were sent, and how many were blocked by our rate limits. These totals don't say who sent anything, in which lobby, or which preset was used. Your mutes are kept only in that browser tab and are dropped when the tab session ends, and your "hide quick-chat" choice stays in your browser until you turn it off or clear site data.

**Pug Bots.** Quick Match may include computer-controlled Pug Bots. Bots are named "Pug Bot · ~NN wpm" (for example, "Pug Bot · ~70 wpm") and always carry a visible "Bot" label. The number is the bot's approximate target pace, not its result. They never count toward stats or rankings and are generated by the server. They don't use any recorded player data, they can't be reported, and they are never counted as people in player counts.

**How long we keep it.** Except for leaderboard scores (see Leaderboards) and player reports (below), lobby and race session data is kept in the server's memory only while your session or room exists, plus a 20-second reconnect window if you drop. After the last player leaves a private lobby or Open Room, the room itself (its code and race settings, with no player data) stays open for up to 60 seconds so someone can rejoin, and then it is deleted. If a host removes a player from a room, the server keeps one-way hashes of that player's IP address and temporary session ID for 10 minutes, so they can't immediately rejoin that room. That session data is not saved to disk, a database or logs.

**Logs.** Our game server doesn't write IP addresses, nicknames, lobby codes, race data or reports to its logs. Its logs contain only startup messages, error reports, overall counts every 5 minutes (for example, how many players are connected), the three anonymous daily quick-chat totals described above, the anonymous solo test totals described under Cookies, analytics and error reporting, and a note, with no details, each time a Quick Match starts. Our hosting providers keep their own technical logs, described below.

## Leaderboards
Ranks shows solo typing test scores that players chose to submit. The 'Show my score on the leaderboard' setting defaults on. After a completed solo test, the score is sent only when you press Submit score. Finishing a test, starting the next one, or leaving the results page doesn't send it. You can turn 'Show my score on the leaderboard' off before you press Submit score. If it's off, the score isn't added to Ranks. If the server adds the score, we then save your randomly assigned nickname, your speed (wpm), accuracy and raw speed, and that test's settings (mode, length, language, punctuation and numbers). Solo scores are lightly checked by our server for impossible speeds, but they aren't otherwise verified. We save these scores in our database, which is hosted for us by Neon on Amazon Web Services servers in Oregon, USA. We don't store your IP address or any other identifier with it. Daily, weekly and monthly boards reset on UTC time and are cleared when each period ends. Scores from an ended period stop showing right away, and the stored rows are deleted automatically, usually within an hour. All-time boards keep scores for up to 12 months. Each board shows only its top 100 scores, and lower scores are deleted. Multiplayer race results are no longer submitted to Ranks from the site. Multiplayer scores submitted earlier may stay in our database, and remain available from our game server, until they expire under these same periods or we remove them. To have a score removed, email us your nickname, board, and the approximate date and score, and we'll find and remove it. We may remove scores that look cheated or that break our rules.

Some pages, like leaderboards and results, connect to our game server to check which features are turned on.

## Player reports
You can report a player in your room for cheating, being away (AFK), a problem with a generated name, or another issue, choosing from a fixed list of reasons. You can't type any text. When a report is made, we keep:
- a report ID;
- the room code;
- the reason chosen;
- the time of the report;
- a **hashed** IP address of the reported player's connection (for IPv6, only the first 64 bits are used). This is a one-way code made with a secret key that is held only in memory, never stored, and destroyed and replaced every 7 days. Without that key it can't be turned back into the IP address, and it can't be linked across 7-day periods.

We don't keep your IP address itself, player IDs, nicknames, messages or any other content with a report. Separately, and only in memory for the rest of the reported player's session, the server keeps a one-way hash of the IP address (or, if no connection address is available, of the temporary session ID) of each player who reported them for cheating, so that it can count distinct reporters; your own session also remembers which players you reported, so you can report each one only once. These are deleted when the session ends and are never written to disk or logs. Reports are kept separately from our server logs and never written to them. We use reports only to prevent abuse, for example to spot repeat cheaters or spam. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in keeping races fair and safe (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). A hashed IP address or session ID is still personal data, so we treat it with the same care. Reports are **deleted automatically after 7 days**. Players repeatedly reported for cheating by different people, or repeatedly flagged by our automatic race checks, may be limited in Quick Match to matches with Pug Bots for the rest of their session. This limit applies to Quick Match only, isn't saved, and ends with the session.

## Steam sign-in (desktop app)
The PuggieType desktop app on Steam can let you sign in with your Steam account. Sign-in happens on Steam's own website in your browser; we never see your Steam password. When you sign in, Steam tells our game server your public Steam ID, and our server asks Steam whether your account owns PuggieType (and any supporter pack) and what your Steam display name is. We use this only to sign you in, unlock what you own, and show your display name if it passes our name filter and you haven't hidden it. Our server does not store or log your Steam ID or display name; it holds them in memory only while handling the sign-in request. We don't sell or share Steam data, or use it for marketing. Our game server runs on Fly.io; see Service providers. Steam data is provided by Valve 'as is', without warranty. Valve's own handling of your data is described in Valve's Privacy Policy (https://store.steampowered.com/privacy_agreement/).

## Accounts (ages 13+)
You can use PuggieType without an account. Accounts are only for people aged 13 and over. If you create one, we process:
- **Sign-in details:** if you use email and password, your email address and password (we never see your password; Google Firebase Authentication stores it only in hashed form). If you sign in with Google, Google gives Firebase Authentication your Google account ID, your email address, whether it's verified, and your Google profile name and picture link. We don't use or copy your name or picture, and we don't receive your Google password, contacts or other Google data. Firebase Authentication also records when your sign-in was created and last used, and Google may log technical data such as your IP address when you sign in.
- **Account record:** a random account ID, a generated pug name, whether your email address is verified, that you passed the age check and when (never your birth year), whether your scores may be listed on leaderboards (off by default, and it can't be changed yet), when you created the account, and when the record was last updated (this changes when your signed-in session is set up with our service, for example when you sign in). The record also has fields we don't use yet, so they hold only their starting value for every account: seconds typed (0), a verified-typist mark and its date (off and empty), and an account status (active for everyone) with a reason and notice reference that stay empty unless the status is changed. Our database does not store your email address. To limit abuse, our database also keeps short-lived request counters for your account ID (how many requests of each kind, such as sign-in, saving or download, you made in the current hour or day; no content, no IP address or email). They are deleted with your account and otherwise within about 3 days.
- **Settings sync:** while you're signed in, our website saves a fixed list of your PuggieType preference settings to your account and restores it when you sign in on another device. Free-text and link settings, such as a custom background link, stay on your device.
- **Personal bests:** while you're signed in, after a finished standard test we save your best speed, raw speed, accuracy and consistency for that test mode, length, language and punctuation/numbers setting, with the date. These are sent from your browser, are not verified, are shown only to you, and are not used for leaderboards.
- **Age check:** we ask for your birth year once, only to check that you're old enough. It isn't sent to our servers or stored; only a yes/no result is used. If the answer means you can't have an account, we delete the sign-in that was just made and keep only a note in your browser (with the time) that stops the question being asked again for 12 months. If that deletion fails, an automatic daily cleanup removes it within about 2 days.
- **Security check:** the sign-in, create-account and age-check steps use Cloudflare Turnstile. When you open one, your browser loads Turnstile from Cloudflare, which looks at signals such as your IP address, browser and device details to tell people from bots, and keeps a small item in your browser. We use the result to protect sign-in and sign-up from abuse. Cloudflare does this for us as our processor; separately, Cloudflare also uses these signals as an independent controller to improve Turnstile, as described in the [Turnstile Privacy Addendum](https://www.cloudflare.com/turnstile-privacy-policy/). Turnstile isn't used to show ads.
- **Account emails:** Firebase sends you an email to verify your address and, if you ask, to reset your password or confirm an email change. These are only account emails, not marketing. We may also use your sign-in email to reply to your requests and to tell you about a decision on your account (see Decisions about your account below); we never use it for marketing.

**Why (legal basis):** to provide the account you ask for, including sign-in, sync, personal bests, account emails, data download and deletion (performance of a contract, GDPR Art 6(1)(b)); to answer your rights requests and give you notices the law requires (legal obligation, Art 6(1)(c)); to keep sign-in and sign-up secure, apply request limits, stop abuse, enforce our Terms (including any decision on your account status) and keep accounts to people aged 13 and over (legitimate interests, Art 6(1)(f)). Where we rely on legitimate interests you can object (see Your rights).

**Who processes it for us:** Google (Firebase Authentication, and Google sign-in if you use it), Cloudflare (Turnstile, and hosting of our accounts service and website) and Neon (our account database, hosted in the US, Oregon). They act as our processors under their data processing terms.

**International transfers:** this data is stored and processed in the United States. For users in the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland, transfers to Google LLC, Cloudflare, Inc. and Neon, LLC (through Databricks, Inc.'s certification) rely on their EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework certifications and its UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. equivalent. Where a certification doesn't cover a transfer (for example, to Neon, Inc., the importer named in Neon's data processing terms), it relies on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (and the UK and Swiss equivalents) in the provider's data processing terms.

**What's in your browser:** while you're signed in, your browser keeps your sign-in in Google's Firebase storage (IndexedDB), a marker that you're signed in ("ptAccountsSession", localStorage), and, if saving settings hasn't finished, your account ID ("ptAccountsSettingsDirty", localStorage). After an under-13 answer it keeps "ptAccountsAgeBlock" (localStorage, 12 months). Cloudflare Turnstile keeps an item in its own frame in your browser's storage. We use these only to sign you in and protect sign-in, not for advertising, so we don't ask for consent for them.

**How long we keep it:** for as long as you have an account. When you delete your account we delete your account record and everything saved to it, such as settings and personal bests, from our database and your sign-in from Firebase straight away; if Firebase can't be reached, we keep only your account ID until it is removed, within 30 days. Our database provider keeps short-term restore copies, and anything you delete drops out of them within about 6 hours; we don't restore deleted accounts. Google and Cloudflare keep limited security logs and backups as described in their own policies.

**Deleting your account:** sign in, open the sign-in page and choose "delete account". You can also email privacy@puggietype.com and we'll do it within 30 days (we may ask you to confirm you control the account).

**Decisions about your account:** if we ever suspend or end your account, or hide or remove a personal best, a person makes that decision, not an automated process alone. We'll tell you why and how to appeal (see our Terms). If you think we got it wrong, email privacy@puggietype.com.

**Download your data:** when signed in, choose "Download my data" to get a JSON file with what our database holds for your account, plus your sign-in email address and sign-in method (email and password, or Google), which come from your sign-in and aren't stored in our database. The file has your account record (every field listed above, including your account ID, pug name, status, when you passed the age check and when the record was last updated), your settings and when they were last saved, and your personal bests. It also has two lists that are empty today because we don't record that data yet: recent results (each with the average and spread of your keystroke timing and the app version saved with it) and runs flagged for review (with the keystroke timings of those runs). Our short-lived abuse counters aren't included. We create the file when you ask and don't keep a copy. You can download it up to 3 times per UTC day; if you reach that limit, email privacy@puggietype.com and we'll send you a copy.

**Your rights:** depending on where you live (including under the GDPR, UK GDPR and the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended), you can ask to access, correct, delete or export your data, to object to or restrict how we use it, and to complain to your data protection authority. Use the in-account download and delete, or email privacy@puggietype.com. To protect the service, in-account actions are limited per account (for example, up to 3 downloads per day), and a request over a limit is refused and you are asked to wait and try again. **Children:** accounts are only for people aged 13 and over, and the age check is a first line of defence, not a guarantee. If you're a parent or guardian and think a child under 13 has an account, email privacy@puggietype.com and we'll delete it.

## Cookies, analytics and error reporting
PuggieType does not load advertising or error-reporting scripts, and does not set advertising cookies. We do not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. If you sign in, we also store the items listed under Accounts (ages 13+); they're used only to sign you in and aren't advertising or analytics.

**Google Analytics 4.** We use Google Analytics 4 (Google LLC) to understand how people use puggietype.com (for example page views, rough geography, and finished solo tests with the test mode, length, language and a rough speed range). Google may process technical data such as IP address, device/browser type, and page URLs on our behalf. For the page address, we send only puggietype.com and the page path, with room codes and profile names replaced. We never send the rest of the address, such as sign-in or password-reset codes or anything after #. If you arrive from a tagged promotional link (for example from our YouTube videos), the page view where you land also includes up to four short campaign labels from that link (source, medium, campaign and content), each only if it is a single word of up to 40 letters, numbers, hyphens or underscores. For the page you came from, we send only that site's address (for example youtube.com), or the page path if it was a PuggieType page. We never send any other query or link details. We configure GA4 with Google signals off and without using Analytics for ads personalization. We ask before GA4 runs, wherever you are. If you reject or don't choose, it doesn't load. Google Analytics 4 sets first-party cookies `_ga` and `_ga_<ID>` only after you accept; they are used to tell browsers and visits apart; they last up to 13 months after your last visit with analytics on. To withdraw, open Privacy and cookie settings and turn analytics off. GA stops right away in that tab, and in other open PuggieType tabs when they reload. PuggieType deletes its GA cookies, and you can also clear cookies in your browser. See [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) and [how Google uses data when you use our partners' sites](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites). You can also use [Google Analytics opt-out](https://tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout) where available.

**Solo test counter.** When you finish a solo typing test, your browser sends our game server the test mode and length (for example, "time, 30"). It sends no cookie, ID, nickname, score or text, and nothing is stored on your device for it. The server only adds one to an anonymous running total for that mode and length, and the totals appear in its daily usage log. It doesn't keep a record of individual tests. To limit abuse, it holds your IP address in memory for about a minute, without logging or storing it. The counter doesn't run if you reject analytics, or in a tab that's on a kids guide or has visited one. Our hosting providers' own technical logs are described below.

Your analytics choice is saved in this browser (localStorage "acceptedCookies") until you change it or clear site data. After a visit to a kids guide, a session-only marker ("puggie-kids-flow") keeps the analytics banner and Google Analytics off in that tab.

Other traffic statistics we use are the aggregate totals Cloudflare produces from its own request data (see above).
